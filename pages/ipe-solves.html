1Introduction to Management

Fall 2024 Q2aManagerial concerns: effectiveness and efficiency

Efficiency = “doing things right”: getting the most output for the least input (means; low resource waste). Effectiveness = “doing the right things”: attaining organizational goals (ends). Managers aim for low waste and high goal attainment together.

EfficientInefficient
EffectiveGoals met at low cost (ideal)Goals met but resources wasted
IneffectiveCheap, fast production of the wrong thingWastes resources and misses goals

Example: a garment factory that sews quickly with little scrap (efficient) but makes styles nobody buys is efficient but not effective.

Fall 2023 Q2aAdministration vs management

AdministrationManagement
LevelTop level (owners, board, top managers)Middle and lower level
FunctionDecides objectives and policies, overall planningExecutes policies, organizes people and resources
NatureThinking, decidingDoing, directing and controlling
FocusWhat to do and whyHow to do it and who does it

In practice the two overlap. Top managers do both, and the four management functions (planning, organizing, leading, controlling) apply at every level.

Spring 2024 Q8aClassify managers by their jobs

By level: first-line managers (supervise non-managerial employees), middle managers (manage first-line managers), top managers (organization-wide goals and plans). By area: functional managers (marketing, production, finance, HR), general managers (oversee a whole unit), line managers (directly in the goal-producing work) vs staff managers (advise and support), project managers.

2Inventory Management (EOQ, price breaks, MRP/ERP)

Fall 2024 Q7aWhy unit price is ignored in EOQ but needed in the quantity-discount model

Basic EOQ assumes no quantity discounts, so the unit price is the same for every order size and the purchase cost P·D is a constant that does not change with Q. Only ordering cost (D/Q)S and holding cost (Q/2)H vary, so the minimum falls at the same Q* whatever P is. With discounts, P depends on Q, so P·D differs between price breaks and must be in the total cost: TC = (D/Q)S + (Q/2)H + P·D. In addition, if holding cost is a % of price, H changes with P.

Fall 2024 Q8aBookstore notebook EOQ

D = 2,000×12 = 24,000/yr   S = 20+20+10 = $50/order   H = 30%×$2 = $0.60/unit/yr   P = $2
Q* = √(2×24,000×50/0.60) = 2000   TC = (24,000/2000)×50 + (2000/2)×0.60 + 24,000×2 = 600 + 600 + 48,000 = $49,200
Reorder point = (24,000/300)×5 = 400 units
Order 2000 notebooks each time; annual total cost ≈ $49,200. Reorder when stock falls to 400.

Spring 2023 Q4b“Cells are miniature versions of product/process layout”: interpret

A group technology (GT) cell pulls together the machines needed for a family of similar parts. Inside the cell, parts flow in a fixed sequence like a product layout (short moves, less WIP, faster rates). The cells themselves sit in a shop whose overall arrangement is still process-like (flexible, general-purpose machines). Hence GT cells are a hybrid layout: small product lines embedded in a process layout, keeping the flexibility of process layouts with much of the efficiency of product layouts. Before GT the flows cross the whole shop; after it, each cell handles its family.

Example: a sheet-metal shop that creates one cell for all bracket-type parts (cut → bend → drill) instead of sending them across separate cutting, bending and drilling departments.

ReferencesClass slides “Chapter 10 – Facility Layout” (Wiley 2007): “Designing Product Layouts” (6 steps), “Hybrid Layouts”, “Group Technology”. Stevenson, Operations Management, Ch. 6 “Process Selection and Facility Layout”: “Designing Product Layouts: Line Balancing”. Learning curves: see topic 10.

Fall 2024 Q1b / Fall 2023 Q1bExpectancy theory (supply-chain employee at XYZ; Partex)

Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence. Expectancy: will extra effort improve my performance? (needs tools, training, authority). Instrumentality: will good performance be rewarded? (clear link, such as a bonus for on-time delivery or lower logistics cost). Valence: do I value the reward? (money, promotion, recognition vary by person).

Employee view: I will work extra if I believe I can do it, the reward is certain and I value it. Employer view (feasibility, Partex): it works if rewards are tied to measurable performance and affordable; problems are subjective perceptions, differences in what people value, and the cost and complexity of individual reward systems. A balanced environment needs fair, transparent, valued rewards plus the resources to perform.

Spring 2024 Q5aEquity theory with an example

People compare their outcome/input ratio (pay, recognition ÷ effort, skill) with a referent’s. Equal ratios → motivated; unequal → tension. Responses: change effort, change outcomes, distort perceptions, change the referent, or quit. Example: two engineers work equal hours, but a colleague with the same experience gets a higher salary → the first engineer reduces effort or demands a raise.

Spring 2024 Q6b / Spring 2023 Q7aLocke’s goal-setting theory (Rahim; Kaniz)

Motivation rises with goals that are specific, challenging but attainable, accepted/committed, and backed by feedback. Vague goals (“study harder”) and unrealistic ones (“perfect GPA”) demotivate.

  • Specific: “raise CGPA from 2.8 to 3.2 this semester; study 2 hours per course per day”.
  • Challenging yet realistic: adjust targets to current habits; break into weekly milestones.
  • Commitment: write the plan, tell a friend/advisor, link to a reward.
  • Feedback: track weekly marks and revise the plan.

Spring 2025 Q3bNovaFrontier is stuck in the chasm: future steps (Nokia as reference)

Chasm: the gap between early adopters (who accept a good product) and the early majority (pragmatists who need proven solutions, standards and a complete ecosystem).   • Fix the ecosystem now: adopt/adapt Android or Windows, or offer an Android-app compatibility layer, developer funding and SDKs for N-Sphere.
  • Pick a beachhead niche (e.g. professional/enterprise users who value its stability) and win it completely, then expand segment by segment.
  • Use references and partners: reviewers, big app makers, carriers/retailers to give the majority social proof.
  • Reposition against Windows/Android on a clear point (security, stability) and keep the loyal customers happy meanwhile.
