Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) – Deep Dive

CSE 4137/4237 · PDF pp. 35–37 (population-based overview), 436–447 (Santosa tutorial, 5-city example), 523–542 (ACO slides), 543–558 (Mandal 2023, asymmetric 4-city derivation). Tags: S25 Spring 2025 · F24 · F22.

1. The idea: ants find shortest paths

PDF pp. 436–437, 523–530

Real ants wander randomly, and on finding food return to the nest laying pheromone. Other ants prefer stronger trails, so a good path attracts more ants and gets more pheromone: positive feedback (indirect communication through the environment, called stigmergy).

  • Two-path experiment (tutorial p. 436): the nest and food are linked by a short (10 m) and a long (20 m) path; ants split 50/50 at first. Ants on the short path finish round trips twice as often, so its pheromone density doubles, and soon almost all ants use it.
  • Evaporation (slide p. 523): pheromone fades, so long paths lose their trail faster and old, early choices do not lock the colony into a poor local optimum. Without evaporation the first paths would stay “excessively attractive”.
  • ACO (Dorigo, 1992) copies this to solve discrete optimization problems, first the Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP). Slides also mention network routing and urban transportation, and it adapts to a changing graph in real time.
ACO in one line: artificial ants build solutions step by step on a graph, choosing edges with probability that grows with pheromone (learned experience) and visibility (heuristic, usually 1/distance); good tours reinforce their edges and evaporation forgets the rest.

2. Model: TSP as a graph

PDF pp. 437, 545

  • Each city is a node; each edge (i,j) carries pheromone τij (learned, changes) and visibility ηij = 1/dij (fixed heuristic: closer is better).
  • Each ant keeps a memory (tabu list) of visited cities, so the feasible set Mk = unvisited cities. The tutorial implements this by setting the visited city’s column of η to 0.
  • Symmetric TSP: dij=dji. Asymmetric TSP (your exams): dij≠dji, so edges are directed and each has its own τ.
  • A tour must visit every city once and return to the start; its length Lk includes the return edge.

3. The four equations

PDF pp. 437–438, 545 (Eq. 8.1–8.5; paper Eq. 1–3)

  1. Transition rule (ant k at city i): pijk = τijα·ηijβ / Σu∈Mk τiuα·ηiuβ for j∈Mk, else 0.
  2. Evaporation (all edges): τij ← (1−ρ)·τij, ρ∈(0,1] is the evaporation rate.
  3. Deposit (ant k on each edge of its tour): Δτijk = Q / Lk if (i,j) is in tour k, else 0. Shorter tour → more pheromone.
  4. Update: τij ← (1−ρ)τij + Σk=1..m Δτijk (Eq. 8.10).
Notation warning: slide p. 534 writes τ(t) = ρτ(t−1) + Δτ, where ρ multiplies the old pheromone (persistence). The tutorial and paper (and your exams: ρ=0.5) use (1−ρ). With ρ=0.5 both give the same number, but write (1−ρ) and say ρ is the evaporation rate.

Elitist variant (tutorial Eq. 8.5, p. 438): only edges of the best global tour get a boost Δτ = c·fbest with f=1/length; larger c → more exploitation. Optional pass over tours (local search/mutation) can follow construction.

4. The algorithm (pseudo-code and flow)

PDF pp. 438, 535–536, 546

  1. Initialize: τij = τ₀ (all 1 in the exams), η = 1/d, choose m, α, β, ρ, Q, number of iterations.
  2. Place ants on start nodes (all on city 1, one per city, or as the question states).
  3. Construct tours: each ant repeatedly picks the next unvisited city by Eq. 1 until all are visited, then returns to the start.
  4. Evaluate: compute Lk for every ant; record the best-so-far tour.
  5. Pheromone update: evaporate every edge, then add Q/Lk on the edges of each ant’s tour.
  6. Repeat from step 2 until the iteration limit (or no improvement). Output the best tour.

Complexity: each iteration costs about O(m·n²). Pheromone stores the colony’s memory; the quality of the final route depends on α, β, ρ and the selection rule.

5. Picking the next city: roulette wheel vs highest probability

PDF pp. 439–443 (roulette), 547–555 (highest probability)

Two conventions appear in your material. Use the one the question states, otherwise say which you use.

  • Roulette-wheel (tutorial): compute probabilities, form cumulative sums, draw random r∈[0,1], choose the first city whose cumulative value ≥ r (fitness-proportional selection). Random numbers are usually given in the question.
  • Highest probability (paper, and how the exam questions are posed): the ant moves to the city with the largest pij. Deterministic, so results are reproducible.
Tutorial example (5 cities, 3 ants all at city 1, τ₀=1, α=1, β=2, d row 1 = 10, 12, 11, 14): τη^β = 0.0100, 0.0069, 0.0083, 0.0051 → sum 0.0303 → p = 0.3299, 0.2291, 0.2727, 0.1683 for cities 2–5; cumulative 0.3299, 0.5590, 0.8317, 1. Random r = 0.6841 < 0.8317 → city 4. From city 4 (set {2,3,5}): p = 0.2147, 0.5965, 0.1887; cumulative 0.2147, 0.8113, 1; r = 0.4024 → city 3. Continuing gives ant 1: 1-4-3-5-2-1, L = 11+9+14+8+10 = 52; ants 2 and 3 give 60 and 60.
Update: evaporation 1→0.5 on all edges; ant 1 adds 1/52 = 0.0192 → 0.5192 on its edges; ant 2 adds 1/60 = 0.0167 (edge 1-4 gets both: 0.5359). Edges used by more ants get more pheromone. MATLAB run (p. 444): best tour 1-4-3-5-2-1, cost 52.

8. Parameters, behaviour and comparison

PDF pp. 438, 440, 534, 545

ParameterRoleExtreme behaviour
αweight of pheromone (learned trail)α=0: ignores pheromone, greedy nearest-city search. Large α (>1): rapid stagnation, all ants follow one path
βweight of visibility 1/d (heuristic)β=0: pheromone only, usually poor results. Large β: greedy, behaves like nearest neighbour
ρevaporation ratesmall ρ: old trails persist, slow adaptation; large ρ: forgets fast, more exploration
Qdeposit scale (Δτ = Q/L)larger Q: stronger reinforcement; Q=1 usually works (p. 438)
mnumber of antsmore ants: better coverage, higher cost per iteration

Characteristics (slide p. 540)

Positive-feedback mechanism, distributed computation with a population of ants, a global data structure (pheromone) that changes dynamically as ants traverse routes, and probabilistic state transitions. Advantages: can beat simulated annealing and GA on TSP-like graphs, adapts continuously, parallel. Limitations: convergence time is uncertain, can stagnate if α is large or evaporation too low, many parameters to tune.

Where it sits in soft computing p. 36

ACO belongs to swarm-intelligence metaheuristics (with PSO, Artificial Bee Colony), a branch of population-based, bio-inspired optimization alongside evolutionary algorithms (GA, DE). Unlike a GA (selection, crossover, mutation on a population of solutions) ACO builds solutions from a shared pheromone memory. Roulette-wheel selection appears in both.

9. Exam kit

Model answer, “Illustrate the algorithm of ACO with an example” (4 marks) F22
1) Initialise τ=1, η=1/d. 2) Place ants. 3) Each ant builds a tour using pij=ταηβ/Σ over unvisited cities. 4) Compute tour lengths. 5) Evaporate τ←(1−ρ)τ and deposit Q/L on each ant’s edges. 6) Repeat; output the best tour. Example: the 3-city case in §6: from A, p(C)=0.6, p(G)=0.4 → tour A-C-G-A of length 25, edges get τ=0.5+0.04=0.54.

Template for “simulate k iterations”

  1. State parameters and the selection rule you use (highest probability or roulette).
  2. Write the η matrix (1/d, directed) and the initial τ matrix.
  3. For each ant and each step: list unvisited cities, compute τη for each, normalize, choose.
  4. Write each tour with the return edge and its total cost.
  5. Show evaporation, then each ant’s deposit Q/L, then the full τ matrix.
  6. Repeat for the next iteration with the new τ. Finish with “best tour = …, cost = …”.

Traps

  • Asymmetric matrix: use d(i→j), not d(j→i); each direction has its own τ.
  • Do not forget the return edge to the start city in length and deposits.
  • Evaporate every edge first, then add deposits; an edge used by two ants gets two deposits.
  • Visited cities are excluded from the probability sum (renormalize over the rest).
  • When one city remains the probability is 1; no calculation needed.
  • Report probabilities to 3–4 decimals and the best tour after all iterations.

Likely short questions

  • How does ACO mimic real ants? (pheromone, positive feedback, evaporation)
  • Why is evaporation needed? (forget poor paths, avoid premature convergence to a local optimum)
  • Role of α and β; effect of ρ and Q.
  • ACO vs GA; where is roulette-wheel selection used?
